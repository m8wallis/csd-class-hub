How to play

One class. One case. Six rules.

  1. Solve it together. Anyone can call out a clue, but the class decides together what to cross off the case board.
  2. Every answer has five parts: Who, What (the act of kindness), Where, When, and Why.
  3. 🔒 Evidence is locked. Solve the riddle, math, code, or Thanksgiving trivia to open it. Every lock has a hint button.
  4. People can be wrong, or modest. Trust data like logs, schedules, and receipts over rumors and what people say about themselves.
  5. Accusations are on the record. The case board tells you how many of your five parts are right, but not which ones. Be sure before you accuse.
  6. Only one answer fits every clue. If something doesn’t fit, keep digging.

    Case file · The Tuesday before Thanksgiving

    Someone did something kind. Nobody signed it.

    Mr. Ray Okafor has been our school’s night custodian for 27 years. He works while everyone else is home: mopping, fixing, locking up.

    On the last day before Thanksgiving break, someone left Mr. Ray a gift and a note signed only “A Grateful Turkey 🦃.” That night Ray left a voicemail at the front office to say thank you, but he has no idea who to thank.

    Principal Hayes wants to thank the Grateful Turkey at the Thanksgiving assembly. She has handed the case to the best detectives in the building: this class.

    • Read the evidence. Solve challenges to open the 🔒 locked pieces.
    • Rule things out on the case board as a class.
    • Accuse: who, what, where, when, and why.

    About 20–30 minutes if everyone talks it out.

    Case closed 🦃

    The Grateful Turkey was Theo Brooks.

    1. Who: Theo Brooks. The door log puts only Theo and Rosie in the library at lunch. Rosie saw a football hoodie, and the receipt and work order both say Theo.
    2. What: Ray’s broken radio, repaired. “Not working in silence,” a radio that broke a month ago and then went missing, a flip phone (so no website), and a new speaker on the receipt.
    3. Where: the library. Ray found it “where I always sit,” and his shift card says he takes his break at the library’s back table.
    4. When: Tuesday at 12:10 PM. The back table was clear through 11:35, the library was locked from 2:28, and Monday night was empty.
    5. Why: Ray found and returned something precious. In October, Ray dug through the bleacher trash for an hour to find Theo’s grandpa’s watch.

    Theo said, “I barely know him.” But Ray had done something for him that he never forgot. Kindness doesn’t need a signature, but noticing it matters.

    Talk about it: Who at school does quiet work that makes your day better? How could you thank them before break?
